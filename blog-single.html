HOW I COLOR HORROR PAGES

By The Colorist

Senior Colorist

Horror comics require a delicate balance. If you use too much color, the tension dissipates. If you use too little, the art becomes muddy and difficult to read. In this tutorial, I'll walk you through my methodology for coloring a horror sequence.

1. The Base Palette

For most horror settings, I start by entirely removing pure whites and bright yellows from my palette. The base is built on desaturated cool grays and deep, bruising purples.

"Darkness in a comic isn't just black ink; it's the absence of warm light."

2. The Accent Color

To make the horror land, you need an accent. Typically, this is a stark, aggressive crimson or an unnatural, sickly green. The key is to use it sparingly. When the accent color appears, it should feel violent.

By keeping the rest of the page subdued, that single splash of red in panel 4 suddenly demands 100% of the reader's attention.
